Why now

Three things changed inside our own multi-site hospital operations, not in an analyst report.
1
First
We already run mixed robot and human capacity across sites, and vendors release new robot types every quarter, so the fleet is mixed whether or not we manage it deliberately.
2
Second
The Shadow AI audit found five tools, and two of them are this product being hand-run: unit spreadsheets tracking which robot actually works for which errand, and supervisor census sheets forecasting tomorrow's demand. Staff built the track record of what works and the demand forecast by hand because nothing else exists.
3
Third
Two enterprise pilots convert in the coming quarter, which sets the window for writing data-sharing and reporting standards into the next fleet contracts.
Honest caveat for the room: the external market timing case is not in the strategy. The why-now here is internal evidence, strong enough to fund a 12-week test, not strong enough to claim a market window.

The economics

At a hospital with 30 inpatient units, a $4,000 base plus $0.40 per verified completion across 86,400 completions/month = $38,560/month, ~$463k/year on the runtime, against ~$3.2M/year of returned time. Value capture ~14.5%, leaving enough on the table that the buyer's business case survives a skeptical CFO.

Blended margin, once per-hospital implementation is amortized over 36 months at a $300k planning figure, is set by density rather than by inference — see below. The margin problem is integration labor, not inference
No predecessor margin — this is 0-to-1. Today the cost is absorbed invisibly into nursing hours: roughly 11.2 nurse-hours per unit per day on off-unit errands
Break-even
~5instrumented units per hospital
Monthly contribution is $4,000 base plus ~$999 per unit against ~$8,333 of amortized implementation. Below five units a site is a services engagement rather than a software deployment, which is the argument for productizing integration before site three
Inference stays cheap by design: rules-based logic handles assignment and AI models only read incoming requests and forecast demand, so 92% of the work never touches an expensive AI model and AI pricing could triple without changing the decision. The cost that matters is integration labor. Against $300k per hospital amortized over 36 months, blended margin runs ~7% at 5 instrumented units, ~36% at 10, ~58% at 20, ~66% at 30. Break-even is 5 units, so we productize integration before site three or every new hospital is a services engagement wearing a software label.

The Risks

What could break this

Trust / Failure
The headline risk is motion reported as completion - an emergency, top-priority specimen, or controlled substance marked delivered that never arrived. The answer is structural, not statistical: nothing is verified without a proof event, under any condition. Restricted payloads require human approval regardless of confidence. Above 0.25% unqualified dispatch, autonomous dispatch halts. A fixed suite of 120 tests, 30 of them deliberately hostile, gates every change.
Scale / Governance
What breaks at 10x is the correction loop, not cost. Escalation starts near 12% and must reach under 6% by month twelve; flat at scale means we hire reviewers linearly with volume. Stated plainly: promoting a correction into a rule is a weekly human ritual today, not an automated system. Autonomy is bounded by what is being carried, no AI agent can change a rule on its own, and patient transport stays out of scope deliberately.
Competitive
The kill scenario is not a faster competitor. It is no fleet vendor signing the reporting standard by month 6 — then the commercial thesis fails even though the internal product works. Nearer in, week 8 has hard gates: under 55% queue adoption, under 90% verified completion, under 12 nurse-minutes returned per shift, or fewer than 60% of errands linkable to a patient record. Any one stops the bet.

The Roadmap

How this moves through time

Horizon 1
Ship · 0-3 months
  • Prove the three assumptions — the data exists (week 3), it links to patient records (week 4), and we are allowed to use it (week 6)
  • Go live on one med-surg unit with three errand types, starting from the rules the unit uses today (weeks 0-6)
  • Capture every override with a reason from day one (weeks 0-3)
  • Weekly rule-update meeting running — corrections become rules (weeks 4-12)
Horizon 2
Validate · 3-9 months
  • Vendor commitments — two fleet vendors sign the reporting standard and share performance data; first scorecard across robot types published to those who contribute
  • Demand forecasting — live clinical schedule feeds tomorrow's errand forecast on the pilot unit
  • Connect to Epic so transport orders arrive automatically and proven completions write back
  • Add a second vendor's robots under the same standard
  • Extend from the unit to sterile processing (surgical case carts)
  • Make hospital onboarding repeatable — reusable integrations plus starting rules
Horizon 3
Explore · 9-18 months
  • Open the shared scorecard to one outside hospital system, with access to the scorecard as the reason to join
  • Explore whether the incident and near-miss record has value to insurers
Kill line for Horizon 2: if no fleet vendor signs the reporting standard by month 6, there are no vendor commitments and the commercial thesis fails even though the internal product works, and we should say so rather than continue.
